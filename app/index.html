How many animals are needed for 80% power?

Move the sliders to see how power changes with the number of animals and the number of cells per animal. Cells come in clusters (one animal), so the share of the variance that lies between animals (the ICC) sets a ceiling that more cells cannot lift.

Question

ICC = between-animal variance / (between-animal + within-animal variance). The chips are generic planning values (low, medium, high); use the slider for any other value.

d = difference in means divided by the standard deviation. 0.2 is small, 0.5 is medium, 0.8 is large.

The curves are computed, not measured. Standard error of the difference (Aarts et al., 2014, Box 3): SE² = 2[1 + (n − 1)·ICC] / (k·n) for two groups of k animals, or [1 + (n − 1)·ICC] / (k·n) for one effect measured in each of k animals. Power is two-sided, α = 0.05, from the noncentral t distribution with 2k − 2 (two groups) or k − 1 (one effect) degrees of freedom. With few animals this is lower than the normal approximation.

Assumptions: roughly normal outcome, equal cells per animal, one effect size. For a within-animal effect, the ICC that matters is how much the effect itself varies between animals, which can differ from the ICC of the raw measure.
